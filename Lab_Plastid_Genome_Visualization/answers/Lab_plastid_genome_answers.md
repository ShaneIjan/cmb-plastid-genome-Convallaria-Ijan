# Questions and Answers

The following questions were answered using the OGDRAW plastid genome map generated for *Convallaria majalis* together with the same annotated GenBank record used in the activity. The answers summarize the main structural regions, gene locations, transcriptional orientation, and GC-content pattern observed in the plastid genome map.

### 1. What is the scientific name of your chosen plant and its plastid genome accession number?

The selected plant is *Convallaria majalis*. Its plastid genome accession number is NC_063623.1.

### 2. What is the total length of the plastid genome in base pairs?

The complete plastid genome is 162,198 bp long.

### 3. Can you identify the LSC, SSC, IRa, and IRb regions on your map? Briefly describe their positions.

Yes. The **LSC** is the large region at the upper part of the map, while the **SSC** is the smaller region near the bottom. **IRa** is located on the lower-right side, and **IRb** is on the lower-left side. Together, these regions form the typical quadripartite structure of the plastid genome.

### 4. Give three examples of genes located in the LSC region.

Three genes located in the LSC region includes `psbD` , `matK` , and `atpH`

### 5. Give at least one example of a gene located in the SSC region.

One gene that can be observed in the SSC region is `ndhF`.

### 6. Give at least one example of a gene that occurs within an inverted repeat region. Is the gene shown more than once because of the duplicated IR regions?

An example is `rrn16`. It occurs in both inverted repeat regions, so it appears twice in the plastid genome because IRa and IRb contain duplicated sequences.

### 7. Choose one photosynthesis-related gene visible on your map. State its gene name and its general biological function.

The `rbcL` gene is involved in photosynthesis and is shown in green on the OGDRAW map according to its functional gene category. It encodes the large subunit of RuBisCO, an enzyme responsible for fixing carbon dioxide during the Calvin cycle.

### 8. What does the direction of the gene arrows or gene orientation tell you about transcription on the plastid genome?

The gene orientation shows the direction in which each gene is transcribed. Genes pointing in opposite directions are transcribed from opposite DNA strands.

### 9. Look at the GC content graph. Does GC content appear uniform across the entire plastid genome, or does it vary among regions?

The GC content appears to vary across the genome. The inner GC graph shows small changes among different regions rather than a completely uniform pattern.

### 10. Why is a graphical plastid genome map useful compared with viewing only the raw DNA sequence or a long list of annotations?

A graphical map makes the genome easier to understand because it shows the locations, orientation, and distribution of genes together with the LSC, SSC, and IR regions. For me, it also helps me understand the genome better because the information is presented in a more organized and visual way. This is easier to follow than reading the full GenBank file itself, which can take more time and effort to understand because of the long list of annotations and sequence information.
